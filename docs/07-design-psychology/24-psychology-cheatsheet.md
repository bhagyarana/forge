# 24 · Psychology Cheatsheet

> **What this is.** All 106 cognitive biases and design principles from growth.design's psychology cheatsheet, reorganized around one question: _"what would a screen-scraping agent actually have to measure to check this?"_ That extra column — **FORGE signal** — is the point of this document. Without it, a psychology cheatsheet is reading material. With it, it is the seed of a check catalogue.
> **What this is not.** Not a spec. [28 · Design & Psychology Critic](28-design-psychology-critic.md) is the spec, and it cites specific rows here by name. This document is the reference the spec is built from.
> **Source.** [growth.design/psychology/cheatsheet](https://growth.design/psychology/cheatsheet), 106 principles across four categories: Information, Meaning, Time, Memory.
> **How to read the FORGE signal column.** Three kinds of answer appear:
>
> - a concrete, structural/geometric measurement FORGE's perception layer can take today or with the [Phase 2](../deferred/design-intelligence.md) capture extension — these become `PSY-xx` checks;
> - "no reliable static signal" — the principle is real but needs longitudinal or interaction data (e.g. return visits, A/B results) FORGE cannot observe from a single autonomous crawl. Listed for completeness and for Track B (§27), not for the check catalogue;
> - a pointer to an existing FORGE mechanism that already produces the relevant fact (e.g. the coverage Critic, the Explorer's affordance map) — these become `LOGIC-xx` checks that cost no new capture.

## Information — how people perceive and decide (1–29)

| #   | Principle                  | Definition                                           | FORGE signal                                                                                                                                                                  |
| --- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Hick's Law                 | More options → harder decisions                      | Count of distinct interactive affordances visible in one state without scrolling; flag states above a threshold (start at 7, tune per target)                                 |
| 2   | Confirmation Bias          | People look for evidence confirming existing beliefs | No reliable static signal — needs content-analysis judgement, out of scope                                                                                                    |
| 3   | Priming                    | Prior stimuli shape the next decision                | No reliable static signal                                                                                                                                                     |
| 4   | Cognitive Load             | Total mental effort to complete a task               | Proxy: steps-to-goal in the Explorer's shortest path to a capability's terminal state, and interactive-element density per state                                              |
| 5   | Anchoring Bias             | First piece of information seen dominates            | Order of price/value elements in the accessibility tree (first vs. last) — informational only, not a defect signal                                                            |
| 6   | Nudge                      | Subtle hints affect decisions                        | No reliable static signal                                                                                                                                                     |
| 7   | Progressive Disclosure     | Complexity revealed later, not all at once           | Ratio of "Optional"/"Advanced"/expandable regions to total form fields — high field count with no disclosure is a `CLR-L` layout flag                                         |
| 8   | Fitts's Law                | Large, close targets are easier to hit               | **`PSY-01`** — bounding-box width×height of interactive elements vs. a minimum touch-target size (44×44px, WCAG 2.5.5), and spacing to the nearest other interactive element  |
| 9   | Attentional Bias           | Thoughts filter what gets attention                  | No reliable static signal                                                                                                                                                     |
| 10  | Empathy Gap                | Emotion's influence on behaviour is underestimated   | No reliable static signal                                                                                                                                                     |
| 11  | Visual Anchors             | Elements that guide the eye                          | Presence/position of directional cues (arrows, numbered steps) relative to the primary CTA — informational                                                                    |
| 12  | Von Restorff Effect        | Items that stand out are noticed more                | **`PSY-02`** — contrast/size delta between the primary CTA and its surrounding siblings; a CTA styled identically to secondary actions fails this                             |
| 13  | Visual Hierarchy           | The order in which elements are perceived            | **`PSY-03`** — heading levels + computed font-size deltas should monotonically track document importance; ties into `DC-03`                                                   |
| 14  | Selective Attention        | Distractions are filtered out during focus           | Count of concurrent unrelated CTAs on a task-critical screen (e.g. checkout) — overlaps `CLR-E`                                                                               |
| 15  | Survivorship Bias          | Failed paths are neglected in design                 | `LOGIC` — cross-reference: does the Explorer's map contain any recorded `haltReason: ERROR` states with no alternate path recorded?                                           |
| 16  | Banner Blindness           | Repeated exposure gets tuned out                     | No reliable static signal                                                                                                                                                     |
| 17  | Juxtaposition              | Close, similar elements read as one unit             | Feeds `DC-07` spacing-rhythm and `PSY-04` grouping consistency                                                                                                                |
| 18  | Signifiers                 | Elements that communicate their own function         | Role/semantics mismatch: a `<div onclick>` with no `role="button"` and no visible affordance styling — accessibility-tree gap, ties to `DC-01`/`DC-02`                        |
| 19  | Contrast                   | Higher visual weight draws attention                 | **`DC-06`** (already spec'd) — WCAG contrast ratio, extended to non-CTA emphasis elements                                                                                     |
| 20  | External Trigger           | The next action is stated in the prompt itself       | Copy check: does a notification/banner element contain an actionable verb? Feeds `CLR-C`                                                                                      |
| 21  | Decoy Effect               | A discardable third option makes another look better | No reliable static signal (pricing-page specific, needs multi-tier comparison)                                                                                                |
| 22  | Centre-Stage Effect        | The middle option in a set is chosen more            | Positional note only when ≥3 comparable options exist side by side (e.g. pricing tiers) — informational                                                                       |
| 23  | Framing                    | Presentation shapes the decision                     | Copy check: loss-framed vs. gain-framed language near a commitment point — informational, feeds `CLR-C`                                                                       |
| 24  | Law of Proximity           | Close elements are read as related                   | **`PSY-04`** — gap between semantically related elements (label↔input, error↔field) vs. gap to unrelated elements                                                             |
| 25  | Tesler's Law               | Simplifying too much shifts complexity to the user   | `LOGIC` — a form with zero inline guidance and a high post-submit error rate (from the Runner's assertion failures) is a candidate flag                                       |
| 26  | Spark Effect               | Small effort barriers increase action                | Field-count on the first screen of a multi-step flow — high count is a `CLR-L` flag                                                                                           |
| 27  | Feedback Loop              | Actions get a visible response                       | **`CLR-R1`** — does a committing action (submit/click) have a detectable resulting state change (new state, message, or DOM mutation) within the Runner's observation window? |
| 28  | Expectations Bias          | Users are anchored to their own expectations         | Ties to `LOGIC` CTA-label/outcome mismatch — a label like "Continue to payment" that lands on a state without payment affordances                                             |
| 29  | Aesthetic-Usability Effect | Attractive design is perceived as more usable        | Composite — this is _why_ the Delight Score in [28](28-design-psychology-critic.md) exists as a single number, not a defect list                                              |

## Meaning — motivation and social context (30–61)

| #   | Principle                   | Definition                                     | FORGE signal                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 30  | Social Proof                | Behaviour adapts to what others do             | Presence check: review counts, testimonials, usage stats near a commitment point — informational, not a defect                                                                     |
| 31  | Scarcity                    | Limited supply raises perceived value          | Presence + **ethics flag**: a countdown/inventory claim with no supporting data anywhere on the page is a `CLR-C` dark-pattern candidate                                           |
| 32  | Curiosity Gap               | Missing information drives seeking behaviour   | No reliable static signal                                                                                                                                                          |

| 36  | Miller's Law                | Working memory holds ~7±2 items                | **`PSY-05`** — item count in navigation menus, dropdown lists, and flat option groups; flag lists ≥9 with no grouping                                                              |
| 37  | Unit Bias                   | One unit feels like the "right" amount         | No reliable static signal                                                                                                                                                          |

| 40  | Reciprocity                 | People reciprocate after receiving value       | No reliable static signal                                                                                                                                                          |

| 42  | Authority Bias              | Expert endorsement carries weight              | No reliable static signal                                                                                                                                                          |
| 43  | Pseudo-Set Framing          | Steps framed as part of a series feel easier   | **`PSY-06`** (Zeigarnik-adjacent) — multi-step flows (≥3 states in one capability) should expose a step indicator; absence is a flag                                               |
| 44  | Variable Reward             | Unpredictable rewards increase engagement      | No reliable static signal                                                                                                                                                          |
| 45  | Group Attractiveness Effect | Grouped items seem more attractive             | Feeds `PSY-04` proximity/grouping                                                                                                                                                  |

| 48  | Self-Initiated Triggers     | Self-set reminders get more engagement         | No reliable static signal                                                                                                                                                          |
| 49  | Survey Bias                 | Survey answers skew socially acceptable        | No reliable static signal                                                                                                                                                          |
| 50  | Cognitive Dissonance        | Holding two conflicting ideas is uncomfortable | `LOGIC` — conflicting copy on the same state (e.g. "Free forever" banner beside a paywalled action)                                                                                |
| 51  | Goal Gradient Effect        | Motivation rises near the goal                 | **`PSY-06`** — same as `#43`; progress indicators near completion should increase in salience, not stay flat                                                                       |

| 57  | Law of Similarity           | Similar-looking elements read as related       | Feeds `PSY-04`/`DC-07`                                                                                                                                                             |
| 58  | Law of Prägnanz             | Ambiguous images are read as simpler forms     | No reliable static signal                                                                                                                                                          |
| 59  | Streisand Effect            | Suppression increases awareness                | Not applicable to UI critique                                                                                                                                                      |

## What became a check, and what didn't

Of 106 principles, **~14 produce a mechanical `PSY-xx`/`CLR-xx` check** (Fitts's, Von Restorff, Visual Hierarchy, Proximity, Miller's, Zeigarnik/goal-gradient, Peak-End, Recognition-over-Recall, feedback loop, and a handful of `LOGIC-xx` consistency checks). The rest are either genuinely unmeasurable from a single autonomous crawl (longitudinal, emotional, or social-context effects) or are **informational annotations** the human-friendly report can mention without scoring — this asymmetry is deliberate and is the same discipline [ADR-017](../decisions/ADR-017-arithmetic-blocks.md) already established for the Coverage Critic: **arithmetic only mints a finding when the underlying fact is directly observable, never a plausible-sounding inference.**
